# SURTR PCB review

Both boards use four copper layers and single-sided component assembly. On the head, the front RF routes reference L2 and the back X output references L3. The layer plots show both inner ground planes. The back route removes the amplified X output from the transformer input area. Two ground vias flank its signal via at 1.2 mm center-to-center. The plated SMA signal hole terminates that route without a separate signal via. In the geometry check, the minimum same-layer gaps from the head coax output traces to input traces are about 13.0 mm for X and 4.7 mm for Y.

The antenna head uses the published JLC04161H-7628 stackup with 0.2104 mm outer dielectric, 1 oz outer copper and 0.5 oz inner copper. The uniform X and Y output traces are 0.34 mm nominal for a 50 Ω target. Specify the stackup and request confirmation of finished geometry with the quote. The dipole arms and filter interconnects have circuit-defined impedances.

The X choke L104 is near the C109 layer transition. The coax-connected branch from the transition to L104 is 7.2 mm. The D103 supply diode sits above and away from the X dipole input trace. The X and Y output routes use gradual bends and measure 57.3 mm and 52.1 mm in planar copper, excluding solder joints and the X via. Their 5.2 mm length difference contributes less than 0.73° at 55 MHz if effective permittivity is at most 4.4. Full-channel phase also depends on the X bias tap, SMA launches, amplifier and filter tolerances.

On the bias tee, the short antenna-side center/shield pairs cross intentionally isolated ground areas at the common-mode chokes. The single-ended uniform-line model applies to the plane-backed sections only. The choke transitions, pad discontinuities, SMA launches, and the X-layer transition remain outside the cross-section model. A VNA measurement of the fabricated boards should cover output match and cross-channel isolation, followed by a powered stability test with realistic antenna and cable terminations. The package-envelope 3D views show placement and connector direction. The silkscreen and placement sheets provide exact designators; the envelope views do not verify part polarity.

In each elliptic section, the through path now follows the row from the second high-pass capacitor to the LNA DC block. The first parallel LC pair straddles that row. The second pair keeps its inductor footprint perpendicular to the first inductor footprint, with the capacitor in the row. Each elliptic shunt capacitor has its own adjacent ground via on the same side of the signal path. The X and Y sections use the same placement and routing, translated by 50 mm.

The nominal SPICE model uses the same schematic and part models for both layouts. A sensitivity sweep adding 0–4 nH equally to the three shunt branches changed the peak transmission across 88–108 MHz by about 1.5 dB and the 30–55 MHz transmission by less than 0.01 dB. The sweep varies shunt inductance only and does not assign inductance values to either PCB. Establish the impact of the new placement with extracted parasitics or matched-port VNA measurements.
